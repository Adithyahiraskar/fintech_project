The problem

Small merchants make high-stakes decisions on gut feeling and a paper ledger

Stock is ordered by habit, regular customers quietly disappear, festival and weather demand arrives unannounced, and short-shelf-life stock expires on the shelf. Existing software records what already happened. It rarely says what to do next.

Stock-outs at the wrong moment

Demand spikes on rainy days, match days, and festival weeks. Orders placed after the spike starts arrive too late, and the sale goes to the shop down the road.

Working capital stuck on shelves

Slow movers get reordered out of habit. Cash that could buy fast-moving stock sits in boxes nobody buys.

Expiry waste

Bread, milk, and packaged snacks have days, not months. Without a daily reminder, expiring stock is discovered after it is unsellable.

Regulars lapse quietly

A customer who shopped every 10 days for a year stops. Nothing in a billing book flags that, and by the time it is visible they have a new shop.

Local signals never reach the order book

Weather, festivals, cricket, school calendars, and society events move demand every week. Most merchants hear about them, then order the same quantities as always.

Analytics tools assume an analyst

Dashboards full of charts do not help a shopkeeper at 7 am with two customers waiting. The answer needs to be a sentence, not a graph.

How it works

From raw transactions to one decision, every morning

The system runs the same chain every day. Each step narrows the output until the merchant receives a small number of actions with the reason attached.

STEP 1

Data

Online and offline sales, stock levels, prices, expiry dates, and customer purchases flow into one store.

STEP 2

Insight

Velocity, trends, slow movers, basket patterns, and customer segments are computed.

STEP 3

Prediction

Next-day and 7-day demand is forecast per product, adjusted for weather and the calendar.

STEP 4

Recommendation

Forecast, stock, shelf life, and margin rules produce specific reorder, discount, and outreach actions.

STEP 5

Action

The merchant gets a WhatsApp voice brief and one tap to accept or dismiss each action.

STEP 6

Measured value

Accepted actions are tracked against what actually sold, so forecast accuracy improves.

Capabilities

Eleven systems, one purpose: the next right action

Every capability exists to answer a question a merchant already asks themselves. None of them exist to make a dashboard look busy.

Photo to product catalog

Photograph a product and the system drafts a name, category, tags, search keywords, description, and basic attributes. The merchant edits and approves before anything goes live.

Removes the typing that stops small merchants from ever listing products online.

One sales ledger, online and offline

Counter sales and online orders write to the same store. Quantity, revenue, cost, margin, and per-product velocity update on every sale.

One number for the business instead of two books that never agree.

Inventory that knows what is coming

Stock, minimum thresholds, supplier details, and historical movement sit next to the forecast, so a low stock level is read against expected demand, not in isolation.

Turns "I am running low" into "order 30 units before Thursday."

Customer intelligence (RFM, explained simply)

Recency, frequency, and value become five plain labels: New, Good, Moderate, At Risk, Inactive. A regular who is overdue against their own purchase interval flips to At Risk automatically.

Shows who is drifting away while there is still time to act.

Acquisition and retention tracking

Record how each customer arrived (walk-in, referral, WhatsApp, campaign), measure which sources actually produce regulars, and get reminders with a suggested message for the ones at risk.

Makes word of mouth measurable instead of assumed.

Demand forecasting, next day and next week

Historical sales, weekday patterns, and seasonality produce a per-product demand estimate: expected units tomorrow, expected peak-day demand, and a recommended order quantity.

Replaces the order-book guess with a number the merchant can adjust.

Context-aware local forecasting

The forecast is then corrected using weather, festival calendars, cricket fixtures, school schedules, and aggregated neighbourhood demand patterns.

Rain, festival weeks, and match days change what sells. The order should change too.

WhatsApp voice morning brief

A 40-second voice note summarises only the highest priority actions: what to order, what to discount, who to contact. The dashboard stays available for detail.

Meets the merchant where they already communicate, without a login.

Pricing recommendations with guardrails

Price suggestions consider demand, stock, velocity, shelf life, and margin, and never go below the merchant's own minimum margin. Every suggestion states the trigger, for example: stock is high, demand is slow, expiry is near, consider 5 to 10 percent off.

Clears ageing stock without quietly eroding profit.

Trends, shelf life, and cross-sell

Rising and falling products are named in plain language, expiring stock is ranked by urgency, and frequently bought together pairs drive combo and placement suggestions such as tea with biscuits or bread with milk.

Each of these adds revenue from stock the merchant already holds.

Local signals

Your sales history is only half of the forecast

History describes the shop. Context describes tomorrow. The engine combines both, then explains which signal changed the recommendation.

Internal signals (the merchant's own data)

  • Sales history by product, day, and channel
  • Weekday and weekend patterns
  • Product seasonality and previous peak days
  • Current stock and open orders
  • Customer purchase intervals and segments
  • Expiry dates and remaining shelf life
  • Price changes and promotion history

External signals (the world outside the shop)

  • Weather forecast: rain, heat, humidity
  • Festival and holiday calendars, national and regional
  • Cricket and other sports fixture days
  • School calendars: term start, exams, holidays
  • Local and community events near the shop
  • Aggregated, anonymised neighbourhood demand patterns
Privacy rule: neighbourhood insights are aggregated and anonymised across merchants. No individual customer's transactions are ever visible to another merchant, and the system collects only the personal data needed to serve that merchant's own customers.

What we measure

Value that shows up in the shop's own numbers

Recommendations are only useful if the effect is visible in a metric the merchant already cares about. These are the metrics the platform tracks from day one.

Stock-out rate

Share of days a product sells out before closing, tracked against forecast accuracy.

Expiry wastage

Retail value of stock written off due to expiry, before and after alerts are acted on.

Dead-stock value

Value of units with no sale in the last 60 days, as a share of inventory value.

Inventory turnover

How fast stock converts to sales, by category, month over month.

Repeat purchase rate and retention

Share of customers returning within their own normal purchase interval.

Average order value

Basket size before and after cross-sell and combo suggestions.

Forecast accuracy

How close predicted demand was to actual sales, per product and per week.

Recommendation acceptance rate

Share of suggested actions the merchant accepts, and the measured outcome of each accepted action.

Targets such as 8 to 12 percent higher sales on peak days are hypotheses to validate through pilot data with real merchants. They are not guaranteed results, and this site does not present them as such.
